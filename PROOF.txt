Exact nonempty union-closure size for a completed Lu--Raz family
Research version, 22 October 2026

This is a mathematical proof accompanying the Lean development, not a statement
of acceptance, global priority, or award eligibility. The underlying construction
and conjectured polynomial are due to Kengbo Lu and Abigail Raz (2024),
arXiv:2405.10639v2, Sections 3 and 5. The proof development used OpenAI Codex.

1. Precise statement and the two source conventions

Let q>=4, x=q-1, h=2q, n=4q, r=3q+1, e0=q+3 and m=2q-3. All sets lie in [1,n],
and [a,b] denotes an integer interval, empty when a>b. Complete the missing source
row S_(2q+1) by {2,4} union [2q+2,4q-2]. Retain all other rows printed in Section 3.
Let cl_+(S) be the unions of NONEMPTY subfamilies. Then

 |cl_+(S)| = (23q^2+35q-42)/2
           = (23x^2+81x+16)/2
           = 23n^2/32+35n/8-21.                              (1)

Thus (1) includes the conjecture's x>=5 range. If empty subfamilies are permitted,
the answer is instead (23x^2+81x+18)/2: every generator is nonempty, so exactly one
new member, the empty set, is added. The source's listed numerical examples use
the nonempty convention, although its written closure definition does not impose
that restriction. We make no claim that these two conventions are identical.

The completion is forced if the other printed rows and the source's assertion
that every coordinate occurs in n/2+1 rows are retained. Indeed, fix a coordinate
t. Sum its indicator over all specified rows. There is just one unknown indicator,
that of S_(2q+1). Subtracting from 2q+1 determines this indicator uniquely. Direct
summation for the displayed completion gives exactly 2q+1 in every column, so it
is the unique possible row. This assertion and the required column summation are
proved in LuRazSubmission.omitted_row_forced and LuRazParametric.exact_frequencies.
It does not assert that the authors have confirmed an erratum. Our statement also
applies to ANY completion satisfying these printed row and frequency conditions;
see LuRazSubmission.every_balanced_completion_count.

2. Generator notation

Split each set into its core, its intersection with {1,2,3,4}, and its tail in [5,n].
Use the following names for the fixed generators. A name used in a tail formula
means its tail only; its core is always as specified here.

 name   core          tail
 Top    {1,2,3,4}     [5,n]
 A      {1}           [5,q+2]
 B      {}            [r+1,n]
 C      {3}           [5,h+2] union {r}
 D      {2}           [h+1,n]
 E      {1,3}         [h+3,n]
 Z      {1,3,4}       [5,h] union {n}
 K1     {2,4}         [h+2,n-2]
 K2     {2,4}         [h+3,n-1]

Define g(a)=h+a-3 for a<=q+3, and g(a)=h+a-4 for a>=q+4.
The J_a generators have core {1,4} and tail [a,g(a)], 5<=a<=h+1.
Let P=[4,h+1] minus {q+2}. Define beta(p)=h+p when p<=q+1, and
beta(p)=h+p-1 when p>=q+3. The L_p generators have core {2,3}; their tails are
[5,p] union [beta(p),n], with the single exception M=L_e0, whose tail is
[5,e0] union [r+1,n-1]. In particular, M does not contain n.
The function beta is a strictly increasing bijection P -> [h+4,n].

These are exactly the source rows: Top=S_0, A=S_(n+2), B=S_(n+1), C=S_n,
D=S_1, E=S_4, Z=S_2, K1=S_(h+1), K2=S_(h+2), J_5=S_3,
J_a=S_(a-1) otherwise, and L_p=S_(beta(p)-1). There are
1+6+2+2m=n+3 rows. No generator is omitted from this notation.

3. Interval reduction lemmas

For a nonempty collection of J generators, its tail is one interval [a,b], with
 5<=a<=h+1, h+2<=b<=n-3, g(a)<=b.                         (J)
Conversely every such interval occurs. All J intervals contain h+1; their union
therefore has its least left endpoint and greatest right endpoint as endpoints.
The map g is nondecreasing and maps onto [h+2,n-3]. Given g(c)=b, either c>=a,
when J_a union J_c realizes the interval, or c<a, when monotonicity forces b=g(a)
and the single generator J_a suffices.

For nonempty L unions, let t and p be the least and greatest selected parameters.
The tail is [5,p] union [beta(t),n], with t,p in P and t<=p, except for t=p=e0,
which gives M. Every pair occurs by taking L_t union L_p. Strict monotonicity of
beta and the separated prefix/suffix recover t,p, so there are T(m)=m(m+1)/2
such tails, including the exceptional M.

A key inequality is
 p+2<=a ==> beta(p)<=g(a)+1,                               (G)
for p in P and 5<=a<=h+1. If p<=q+1 and a<=q+3, this is h+p<=h+a-2.
If p<=q+1 and a>=q+4, g(a)+1>=3q+1>=beta(p). If p>=q+3, a>=p+2>=q+5,
and g(a)+1=h+a-3>=h+p-1=beta(p). Thus a J interval and an L tail cannot retain
both a left gap and a right gap. The missing final point of M must still be kept.
Adding A or B fills gaps and never creates new ones.

Union only adds core coordinates. The generators containing 4 also contain 1 or 2,
so cores {4} and {3,4} never occur. We count the other fourteen cores below.

4. The six small cores

Their complete tail lists are:
 {}: B;
 {1}: A, A union B;
 {2}: D;
 {1,2}: A union D;
 {3}: C, C union B;
 {1,3}: E, A union E, [5,n], C, C union B.
Here the tail C in the last line is realized by A union C. Inspection of the
allowed core-subsets, using B subset D,E, A subset C, and C union E=[5,n], proves
coverage. The endpoints and isolated points distinguish the entries. Their counts
are respectively 1,2,1,1,2,5. These statements are proved in LuRazSmallResults.

5. Core {2,3}

The allowed generators are B,C,D,L. The complete, disjoint groups of tails are:
 (i) all T(m) L-union tails above;
 (ii) [5,e0] union [r+1,n], one tail;
 (iii) [5,p] union D for p in P, p<=h-1, together with [5,n], m-1 tails;
 (iv) [5,h+2] union {r} union [beta(t),n], t in P except e0, m-1 tails;
 (v) [5,h+2] union [r,n-1], one tail.
They are realized by L_t union L_p; M union B; D union L_p and C union D;
C union L_t; and C union M, respectively.
For coverage, adding B to an L union only creates (ii) when M was alone; all other
changes are already ordinary L unions with adjusted minimum parameter. Adding D
gives (iii), and adding C gives (iv) or (v). The beta values at q+1 and e0 become
the same after adding r, explaining the deletion of e0. With no L, cores force
C and D, giving the full tail. Prefix endpoints, suffix starts, and the point n
distinguish all groups. The count is T(m)+2m=2q^2-q-3.

6. Core {1,2,3}

Allowed generators are A,B,C,D,E,L. Apply optional A and E to all tails in section 5.
This covers the class: combinations using E and D without C or L have the same
tail as D union L_4 before adding E; combinations with neither C nor L must use E,D.
Adding A to (i) replaces p by max(p,q+2). The low triangular block collapses to
q-2 values of t, giving T(m)-T(q-2)+(q-2) tails, still with M exceptional.
Group (ii) contributes one; D-type tails contribute q-1; C-type tails contribute m.
These are disjoint, totalling (3q^2+q-6)/2.
Adding E creates exactly [5,p] union [h+3,n], for p in P union {q+2}, a total m+1,
and the as-yet unlisted D-type tails with p=4,...,q+1, a total q-2. All C-type
tails become full. Distinct prefix and suffix endpoints distinguish these groups.
The total is (3q^2+7q-14)/2. See LuRazCore123Results for the formal enumeration.

7. Core {1,4}

Only A,B,J are allowed and at least one J is required. Subject to (J), the six
disjoint groups are:
 [a,b]                                      2(q-1)(q-2)
 A union [a,b], a>=q+4                       T(q-2)
 [a,b] union B, b<=3q                        T(q-1)+1
 [a,n], 5<=a<=h+1                           2q-3
 A union [q+4,3q] union B                    1
 A union [a,n], q+4<=a<=h+1                  q-2.
Adding A either merges the left endpoint to 5 or retains a left gap. Adding B
either merges the right endpoint to n or retains a right gap. Both gaps can
remain only when a=q+4,b=3q. The largest J endpoint n-3 is at least r because q>=4,
so the merged-suffix forms are all attainable. For the first group, summing the
available b values in the two ranges of a gives 2(q-1)(q-2); the next two sums are
the displayed triangular counts. Gap endpoints and membership of n distinguish
the six groups. Their sum is 3q^2-5q+2.

8. Cores {2,4} and {1,2,4}

For {2,4}, the allowed generators B,D,K yield exactly
 [h+2,n-2], [h+3,n-1], [h+2,n-1], [h+2,n], [h+3,n], D.
The last tail is obtained with D and at least one K. These six tails are distinct.
For {1,2,4}, if J is used, it joins K or D and gives
 [a,t], 5<=a<=h+1, t in {n-2,n-1,n};
 A union [a,t], q+4<=a<=h+1, t in {n-2,n-1,n}.
The endpoints t are realized using K1,K2,K1 union B. If no J is used, A is required;
adding it to the six preceding K/D tails gives five new tails, since A union D
already occurs with a=h+1,t=n in the second group. Distinct left-gap endpoints and
right endpoints give uniqueness. The count is 3(2q-3)+3(q-2)+5=9q-10.

9. Core {1,3,4}

Allowed generators are A,B,C,E,J,Z. Write Q=[h+2,n-3]. The disjoint groups are
 [a,n], 5<=a<=h+1                            2q-3
 A union [a,n], q+4<=a<=h+1                  q-2
 [5,b] union {r}, b in Q except r-1          2q-5
 [5,b] union {n}, b in Q                     2q-4
 [5,b] union {r,n}, h+2<=b<=r-2              q-2
 [5,b] union B, h+2<=b<=r-1                  q-1
 [5,b] union [r,n], h+2<=b<=r-2              q-2
 Z, Z union B, Z union E                     3.
With J and E the suffix reaches n, giving the first two groups. With C or Z, the
J interval joins the existing prefix, leaving only b. In the C case b=r-1 and b=r
give the same set, so delete r-1. Adding Z or B gives precisely the next groups;
larger endpoints merge to full tails already listed. With no J, Z is forced and
the remaining options reduce to the last group or previously listed tails.
For attainability choose J unions [5,b] via (J) and add C,Z,B as indicated; the
first two groups use E union J_a, optionally A. The position of n, isolated r,
and consecutive-block endpoints distinguish the groups. The sum is 10q-16.

10. Core {2,3,4}

Allowed generators are B,C,D,K,L; at least one K is needed, along with C or L.
The complete disjoint tail groups are
 [5,p] union [t,n], p in P, t in {h+2,h+3}     2m
 [5,p] union D, p in P, p<=h-1                 m-2
 [5,e0] union [t,n-1], t in {h+2,h+3}          2
 [5,n-2], [5,n-1]                             2
 [5,n-2] union {n}                            1.
K joins the suffix of ordinary L; for M add B when the target includes n. With
p=h+1 and K1, the isolated missing point n-1 gives the last group; adding L_h
fills it without extending the prefix. D gives the second group or a previously
listed full tail. C joins K and gives the two long intervals, or the last group
if n is also supplied. These arguments give coverage and witnesses. Prefix and
suffix endpoints, membership of n, and the isolated last gap distinguish the
groups. Their sum is 2m+(m-2)+2+2+1=6q-6.

11. The full core {1,2,3,4}

Partition by membership of n,h+1,h+2 and by whether the left prefix has a gap.
This partition is disjoint before any counting.

(a) n absent. The possible tails and counts are
 [5,n-2], [5,n-1]                                     2
 [5,b] union [r+1,n-1], h+2<=b<=r-1                    q-1
 [5,e0] union [a,n-1], q+5<=a<=h+1                     q-3
 [5,e0] union [t,n-1], t in {h+2,h+3}                  2
 [5,b] union [r,n-1], h+2<=b<=r-2                      q-2.
Only A,C,J,K,M are allowed. With no M, the core requires C,K, giving the first
line. With M but no C, (G) allows a left gap or right gap, giving the second/third
lines or [5,n-1]; using K without J gives the fourth. With C and M, r joins the
suffix and the last line remains, or the full interval. J unions [5,b] supply
the adjustable endpoints. The total is 3q-2.

(b) n present, h+1 absent. The tails are
 [5,p] union [t,n], 4<=p<=h, t in {h+2,h+3}            4q-6
 [5,h] union [a,n], h+4<=a<=n-1                        2q-4
 [5,h] union [h+2,n-2] union {n}                       1.
Top,C,D,J,L_(h+1) are excluded. With K, the suffix starts h+2 or h+3, except for
the last missing-n-1 case Z union K1. All prefix endpoints 4,...,h are available
from P plus A's endpoint q+2, and E provides core 1,3 when needed. Without K,
the core requires Z and L, forcing the second group. Their count is 6q-9.

(c) n,h+1 present, h+2 absent. Only L_(h+1) supplies h+1 without h+2, so the
prefix is [5,h+1]. The tails are [5,h+1] union [a,n], h+3<=a<=n, count 2q-2.
Use Z union L_(h+1) for core 4 and adjust the suffix using L; use E for a=h+3.

(d) n,h+1,h+2 present, 5 absent. A,C,Z and L_p for p>=5 are excluded. To supply
core 3 one needs E or L_4. If J is used, its interval joins that suffix; otherwise
D supplies h+1. The tails are exactly [a,n], 6<=a<=h+1, count 2q-4, attained by
J_a union L_4.

(e) n,h+1,h+2,5 present, but [5,h+2] not full. C and Z are impossible. By (G),
a surviving left gap forces all pieces to its right to join. If no L is used,
E supplies core 3 and gives the same result; without J, D supplies h+1 unless
L_(h+1) has already filled the prefix. Thus the tails are precisely
 [5,p] union [a,n], 5<=p<=h-1, p+2<=a<=h+1.
For p in P take L_p union J(a,n-3) union B, where J(a,n-3) denotes an attainable
J union from (J). If p=q+2 take A union L_(q+1) union J(a,n-3) union B instead.
Including B is harmless and fills n in the exceptional p=e0 case. Omitting B
there would give an invalid witness; the present proof and Lean code include it.
The gap recovers both parameters. Summing h-p choices over p=5,...,h-1 gives
(h-5)(h-4)/2=(2q-5)(q-2).

(f) n and the full prefix [5,h+2] present. The tails are
 [5,n]                                                            1
 [5,b] union [c,n], h+2<=b<=n-2, b+2<=c<=n                         (2q-3)(q-1)
 [5,b] union {r} union [c,n], h+2<=b<=r-2, r+2<=c<=n               (q-2)^2.
Each J or K interval joins the existing prefix, so these intervals only extend
its endpoint. All remaining non-isolated pieces join a suffix ending at n; if
M is selected, its n-1 endpoint joins the already present n. The only possible
additional isolated point is C's r. Merge adjacent pieces to obtain exactly the
three listed normal forms. Top attains the first. For the second, b<=n-3 is
attained by J(5,b) and the L whose suffix starts at c; add B only if that L is M.
For b=n-2 the required c=n is attained using J_5,K1,L_(h+1). The third is attained
with C,J(5,b),L_p where beta(p)=c; here c>=r+2 excludes M. Gaps and isolated r
distinguish the forms and recover endpoints. The second count is T(2q-3), and
the last count is (q-2)^2.

The full-core count, summing (a)--(f), is
 (3q-2)+(6q-9)+(2q-2)+(2q-4)+(2q-5)(q-2)
 +1+(2q-3)(q-1)+(q-2)^2 = 5q^2-5q+1.
LuRazFullJL proves the general four-endpoint J/L reduction. LuRazFullFixed proves
closure of the fourteen full-core standard forms under all eight fixed non-Top
generators. LuRazFullRawParts reduces arbitrary source selections to optional J,
optional L and fixed flags. FullCoverage, FullExact, FullUnique, FullEnumeration
and FullCount verify respectively coverage, attainability, uniqueness, enumeration
and count. Thus arbitrary-length source selections, not just pairs, are covered.

12. Sum over cores and connection to actual sets

 core             count
 {}               1
 {1}              2
 {2}              1
 {1,2}            1
 {3}              2
 {1,3}            5
 {2,3}            2q^2-q-3
 {1,2,3}          (3q^2+7q-14)/2
 {1,4}            3q^2-5q+2
 {2,4}            6
 {1,2,4}          9q-10
 {1,3,4}          10q-16
 {2,3,4}          6q-6
 {1,2,3,4}        5q^2-5q+1.

Different cores are disjoint. Each listed tail is attained, every generated tail
is listed, and each list has distinct members. The sum is (23q^2+35q-42)/2.
Substitute q=x+1 and n=4q to obtain (1).

In Lean, source elements are ordinary predicates on Fin(4q), and all row indices
are Fin(4q+3). LuRazFiniteClosure transfers the tail classification to these sets
and proves injectivity on supported predicates. The main theorem gives a Nodup
list with membership equivalent to being generated from the original source and
the stated length. LuRazSubmission.nonempty_subfamily_iff proves equivalence with
arbitrary nonempty predicate-selected subfamilies; this is not a bounded-list
restriction or an unproved identification with encoded sets. This completes the
proof of the precisely stated mathematical result.

13. Scope and verification

The theorem proves no claim of global minimality or Frankl's original conjecture.
It does not establish original-contributor priority or the acceptance of a prize
problem. The formal proof uses Lean 4.35.0 and bundled Std, without Mathlib.
Proof declarations are audited for transitive axioms, and the permitted standard
axioms are propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. See the reproduction report
for the actual build, axiom audit, kernel-replay level and finite cross-checks.
Finite checks support error detection and do not replace any parameter theorem.
